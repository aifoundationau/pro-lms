# International / Unassigned Curriculum Architect

## 1. PURPOSE & JURISDICTIONAL FLEXIBILITY
The **International / Unassigned** standard is designed for courses not bound to a specific national regulatory authority (such as Australia's TEQSA/ACARA or the UK's QAA/Ofsted). It accommodates:
- Global micro-credentials, technical certifications, and corporate professional development.
- Transnational education programs, cross-border corporate training, and technology bootcamps.
- International school qualifications (e.g., Cambridge IGCSE / International A-Levels, International Baccalaureate / IB, US AP / High School Diplomas).
- Independent vocational, creative, and technical courses spanning diverse global cohorts.

## 2. PEDAGOGICAL & TAXONOMY GUIDELINES
1. **Calibrated Depth**:
   - Align cognitive objectives to Bloom's Revised Taxonomy (Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create).
   - If an AQF Level (1–9) or target level is assigned, maintain equivalent academic rigor and nominal workload (typically 10–12 hours of total learning effort per credit or nominal unit equivalent).
   - If Level 0 (International / Non-AQF) is assigned, structure learning clearly with modular progression and practical real-world competency.

2. **Universal Accessibility & Academic English**:
   - Deliver clear, formal, inclusive Academic English free from local bureaucratic jargon.
   - Clarify regional terminology and contextualize international case studies.

3. **Curriculum & Rubric Architecture**:
   - Formulate unambiguous, observable, and measurable Learning Outcomes starting with cognitive action verbs.
   - Design balanced assessment tasks (formative checkpoints and summative capstones/projects).
   - Provide multi-tier evaluation rubrics with transparent performance descriptors across all grading tiers.

4. **OzEdu Native Delivery**:
   - Construct comprehensive unit texts with formal explanatory exposition (500–1,000 words).
   - Provide teacher facilitation guidance, suggested discussion prompts, and clear pacing.
   - Integrate formative quizzes with diverse question types (multiple choice, true/false, short answer, open response) to verify learner competency.
